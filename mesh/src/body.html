Neuron Parity 4-bit ALU The idea

A layer of a neural network, made of light

Eight numbers go in as light. A mesh of 28 interferometers mixes them, and photodiodes read what comes out: one layer of a network, 64 multiply-adds, in one pass of light. Two passes, with a little electronics in between, read handwritten digits. Draw one below. The chip's answers on this page come from a simulated chip that was calibrated and programmed using nothing but its own three outputs.

What one pass of light does

  1. Inputs. A 1-to-8 splitter feeds eight Mach-Zehnder encoders. Each has a heater on both arms, driven in opposite directions (push-pull), so it sends on cos(Δ/2) of its light at a fixed phase: any value from −1 to +1, sign included.
  2. The mesh. Eight columns of 2 × 2 interferometers, 28 in all, each with an internal phase θ and an input phase φ. With its 56 heaters set, the mesh multiplies the eight input fields by an 8 × 8 unitary matrix U: every output is a weighted sum of every input, with complex weights.
  3. Detection. A photodiode reads |Uk · x|², the squared magnitude of row k times the input. That square is the network's nonlinearity in layer 1, and with a constant on the eighth input it contains terms linear in the features too.
  4. Three rows at a time. The test rules for this fabrication run allow one laser coupler and three detector couplers per circuit, so only outputs 7, 0 and 1 reach a fibre. A layer is read in pages of three rows: the mesh is set so the wanted rows land on those three outputs. Seven hidden units take three settings, ten digit scores take four. A process with on-chip photodiodes would read a whole unitary in one pass.
  5. Between layers. Electronics turn the seven intensities into z = clip(a·h + b, 0, 1) and send them, with a constant, back in as layer 2's inputs. The largest of the ten detected scores is the answer.

The chip

What is simulated

  • The circuit is read from the SiEPIC extraction of the drawn layout and solved with the EBeam PDK's component models at nine process corners. Reflections in the splitter tree and encoders are solved exactly; the forward model agrees with the full scattering solution to of full scale.
  • Each die gets random phases on every waveguide, ±10% heater efficiency, thermal crosstalk along the drawn paths, 16-bit drivers and detector noise.
  • Calibration and programming see only the three detector readings.

What it is not

  • The features (7 principal components of the 28 × 28 image) are computed in electronics, and that takes more arithmetic (5,488 multiply-adds) than the two optical layers (136).
  • The heaters are thermal: reprogramming takes microseconds, and the speed study assumes fast modulators at the encoders.
  • The weights are unitary and complex, read as squared magnitudes; this is not a general real-valued matrix.
  • Optical meshes for neural networks are not new (MIT 2017, Lightmatter and others). The new part here is the full chain from verified layout to calibrated network, in the open.
